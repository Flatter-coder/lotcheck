The Reviews vs MSRP

Volume 1 · Issue 07 July 2026 16 dealerships surveyed

Two things describe a new-car deal, and they don't always travel together. How is the dealer priced? — how far its listings sit below the manufacturer's sticker. And how is the dealer rated? — what hundreds of Google reviewers report after signing. This report plots every local dealer on both axes at once: price versus MSRP against Google review score.

How to read it the four quadrants

The two midlines split the field into four. The horizontal line is the MSRP line: dealers above it list over sticker, dealers below it discount. The vertical line near 3.5★ separates well-reviewed dealers from poorly-reviewed ones. Read the corners, not the middle.

Bottom-right · below MSRP, higher-rated
Below MSRP and higher-rated. Listings sit under sticker and review scores are high — the two signals point the same way.
Top-left · above MSRP, lower-rated
Above MSRP and lower-rated. Listings sit over sticker and review scores are low. Added fees and low scores tend to cluster here together.
Top-right · higher-rated, above MSRP
Higher review scores, but the listings sit over sticker. High ratings paired with above-MSRP pricing.
Bottom-left · below MSRP, lower-rated
Below MSRP, but review scores are lower. Low prices paired with lower ratings.

In the bottom-right, both signals point the same way — below MSRP and higher-rated. In the other quadrants the two signals diverge: either the price or the review score is lower. A dealer sitting low and to the right scores on both axes at once. As the live feed above shows, a dealer can cross into that corner when its reviews improve or its listings drop.

Methodology & honesty note what's real, what's not

Demo data — fictional dealers · simulated drift

Every dealership, rating, review count and price figure in this report is illustrative sample data, generated for a design preview. The dealer names are fictional placeholders — Riverside Ram, Elbow River Audi and the rest are inventions, and none is attached to any real business's rating. The drifting motion in the matrix above is a simulated review stream on a ~2.6-second tick, not live data changing.

In production, nothing on either axis is fabricated. The Google review score and review counts come straight from LotCheck's get-dealer-sentiment pipeline, which reads real Google reviews for each dealer. The MSRP-deviation axis is computed by comparing real scraped dealer listings against the verified msrp_catalog — the same catalog that powers LotCheck's price checks. A dot's vertical position is a measured price gap, not an opinion; its horizontal position is a measured reputation, not a guess.
