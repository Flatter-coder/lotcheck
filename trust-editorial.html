The Reviews vs MSRP

Volume 1 · Issue 07 July 2026 14 dealerships surveyed

Two things describe a new-car deal, and they don't always travel together. How is the dealer priced? — how far its listings sit below the manufacturer's sticker. And how is the dealer rated? — what hundreds of Google reviewers report after signing. This report plots every local dealer on both axes at once: price versus MSRP against Google review score.

Every dealership plotted by its Google review score (left→right, 1.0–5.0★) against how far its listings sit above or below MSRP (top→bottom). Dot size ≈ inventory on the lot. The bottom-right is below MSRP and higher-rated.

How to read it the four quadrants

The two midlines split the field into four. The horizontal line is the MSRP line: dealers above it list over sticker, dealers below it discount. The vertical line near 3.5★ separates well-reviewed dealers from poorly-reviewed ones. Read the corners, not the middle.

Methodology & honesty note what's real, what's not

Demo data — fictional dealers

Every dealership, rating, review count and price figure in this report is illustrative sample data, generated for a design preview. The dealer names are fictional placeholders — Riverside Ram, Elbow River Audi and the rest are inventions, and none is attached to any real business's rating.

In production, nothing on either axis is fabricated. The Google review score and review counts come straight from LotCheck's get-dealer-sentiment pipeline, which reads real Google reviews for each dealer. The MSRP-deviation axis is computed by comparing real scraped dealer listings against the verified msrp_catalog — the same catalog that powers LotCheck's price checks. A dot's vertical position is a measured price gap, not an opinion; its horizontal position is a measured reputation, not a guess.
